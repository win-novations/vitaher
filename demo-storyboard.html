Scene 1

The World Before

Open with what the audience already lives through every day. Make the pain visceral before offering the cure.
0:00 – 1:30  ·  90 seconds

Stage Direction

The presenter's screen shows a deliberately cluttered desktop: four overlapping Excel windows, an Outlook inbox with 47 unread quality-related emails, a shared drive folder open with dozens of Word documents named SOP_ServiceDelivery_v3_FINAL_FINAL2.docx, and a Teams chat thread where someone is asking "Has anyone seen the latest audit report for Johannesburg?"

Hold this screen for five full seconds before speaking. Let the committee recognise their own reality.

This is the state of quality management at ABC Services today.

Audit findings live in spreadsheets that three people have different versions of. Corrective actions are tracked in email threads that get buried within a week. SOPs sit in shared folders where nobody is sure which version is current. Training records are kept in binders at each centre — and nobody at head office can tell you, right now, which staff certifications expired last month.

When the executive team asks "What is our quality performance across the business?" — it takes the quality team two to three weeks to manually compile a report from 25 separate centres. By the time it reaches this table, the data is already stale.

On-Screen Pain Points

Scattered Documents
SOPs across shared drives, email attachments, and local machines. No version control. No acknowledgement tracking.
Email-Driven Actions
Corrective actions assigned by email. No visibility on completion. No escalation when overdue.
Spreadsheet Audits
Audit findings recorded in Excel. No link to corrective actions. No trend analysis. Manual follow-up.
Invisible Risks
No centralised risk register. Risks live in individual managers' heads. No enterprise view.
Delayed Reporting
Weeks to compile a quality report. Data manually gathered from each centre. Stale by the time it's read.
Training Blind Spots
Paper-based training records. No central visibility. Expired certifications discovered only during external audits.
Key Demo Moment
Open a real spreadsheet with mock audit data. Scroll sideways to show 40+ columns. Ask the room: "Can anyone tell me, from this spreadsheet, which ISO clause has the most recurring findings across the Northern Region?" Hold for three seconds. The silence is the point.

The problem is not that your teams lack diligence. They work hard with these tools every day. The problem is that spreadsheets and email were never designed to run a quality management system across 25 centres. You are managing a national quality operation with tools built for personal productivity.

That changes today.

Transition — Screen fades to the ABC Quality Platform login

Scene 3

Module Walkthrough

Move briskly. Executives do not need to see every field — they need to understand the scope and capability of each module in a single glance.
2:15 – 4:15  ·  2 minutes

Demonstration Approach

Navigate through each module by clicking it in the left navigation. Spend 10–15 seconds on each, showing one defining screen and delivering one sentence that explains the business value. Do not open forms or demonstrate data entry — that detail belongs in a separate workshop.

Document Control ISO 7.5

Show the controlled document library. Point out the version column, the status badges (Draft, In Review, Approved, Obsolete), and the acknowledgement completion percentage bar beside each document.

Narrate: "Every document has one version of truth. Everyone who needs to read it has confirmed they've read it — and we can prove it."
SOP Management ISO 8.1

Open one SOP record. Show the structured steps, the linked competency requirements, and the "Impact of Change" section. Highlight the linked training records at the bottom.

Narrate: "When this SOP changes, the system automatically identifies who needs retraining — no manual cross-referencing."
Internal Audits ISO 9.2

Show the audit list with status badges (Scheduled, In Progress, Report Submitted, Closed). Open one completed audit to show the checklist, the photo evidence, and the auto-generated findings linked to NCs.

Narrate: "Auditors capture findings on a tablet on-site. Photo evidence, clause mapping, and corrective actions — all in real time."
Non-Conformances ISO 10.2

Show the NC register with severity colour-coding. Open one NC to show the timeline: raised → investigated → root cause → containment → CAPA link. Highlight the 5 Whys analysis embedded in the record.

Narrate: "Every non-conformance has a root cause analysis, a corrective action, and an effectiveness review — all tracked, all auditable."
CAPA ISO 10.2

Show the CAPA pipeline view — a kanban-style board with columns: Planning, Awaiting Approval, In Progress, Verification, Closed. Show that each CAPA links back to its source NC and forward to its effectiveness review.

Narrate: "CAPAs don't disappear into email. They sit here until they're proven effective — at 30, 60, and 90 days."
Risk Management ISO 6.1

Show the risk heat map — a 5×5 matrix with plotted risk dots, colour-coded by category. Click one risk to show the treatment plan, the review schedule, and the linked quality objective it threatens.

Narrate: "Risks are no longer in people's heads. They're assessed, treated, reviewed, and visible at every level of the organisation."
Training & Competency ISO 7.2

Show the training compliance matrix: a grid of staff names vs required competencies, with green/red/amber cells. Filter to one centre. Point out the expiry warning badges.

Narrate: "At a glance — who is qualified, who needs retraining, and whose certifications expire this month. Across every centre."
Supplier Quality ISO 8.4

Show the approved supplier list with scorecard results and status (Approved, Conditional, Suspended). Open one supplier to show their NC history and performance trend chart.

Narrate: "Every supplier has a performance score based on delivery, quality, and response to non-conformances. No more gut feel."

Narrate: "Complaints are automatically acknowledged, investigated, resolved, and followed up — with customer satisfaction measured at the end."

Show the management review screen with the auto-assembled input pack: audit summary, NC trends, CAPA status, complaint analysis, objective scores, risk changes. Point out the AI-generated executive summary at the top.

Narrate: "The platform assembles every input required by ISO 9.3 automatically. Your Head of Quality no longer spends two weeks preparing for this meeting."

Scene 4

Workflow in Action

This is where the platform proves it is not just a database with forms. Show the automation chain end-to-end. Every step should either happen automatically or take one click.
4:15 – 5:30  ·  75 seconds

Stage Direction

The presenter role-plays as a Centre Manager at the Johannesburg centre. They raise a new Non-Conformance and then show the cascade of automated actions that follow. Use a split-screen or switch between browser tabs to show notifications arriving in real time.

Let me show you what automation looks like in practice. I'm going to raise a non-conformance right now, and I want you to watch what the system does — without anyone asking it to.

NC Raised by Centre Manager
The presenter completes a short form: selects the centre (Johannesburg), chooses a category (Service Delivery — Process Deviation), writes a two-sentence description, uploads a photo, and clicks Submit. The system assigns reference NC-2026-0847.
User action — 30 seconds
AI Auto-Classification
Within two seconds, AI Builder analyses the description and suggests: Severity = Major, ISO Clause = 8.5.1 (Control of production and service provision). The presenter confirms the suggestion with one click.
Automatic — AI Builder
Instant Notification
Switch to a second browser tab showing Microsoft Teams. An Adaptive Card has already appeared in the Regional Quality Manager's Teams channel: "New Major NC raised at Johannesburg — NC-2026-0847. Review required." The card has View and Assign buttons.
Automatic — Power Automate + Teams
Regional Manager Assigns Investigation
The presenter clicks the Assign button on the Teams card. A dropdown shows the centre's process owners. They assign the investigation to the relevant Process Owner, who receives their own notification immediately.
User action — one click
CAPA Auto-Created
Because this NC is classified as Major, the system automatically creates a linked CAPA record, pre-populated with the NC details. A new notification goes to the Centre Manager: "CAPA-2026-0312 created. Action plan required within 5 business days."
Automatic — Business Rule + Power Automate
Reminder at Day 3
If no action plan is submitted within 3 days, the system sends a reminder to the Centre Manager via Teams and email. A yellow "Approaching SLA" badge appears on the CAPA record.
Automatic — Scheduled Flow
Escalation at Day 7
If still no action plan at day 7, the system escalates to the Regional Quality Manager. A red "SLA Breached" badge appears. The Regional QM sees it on their dashboard under Overdue Actions.
Automatic — Escalation Flow
Evidence Uploaded & Verification
Once actions are completed, the Process Owner uploads evidence of completion. The Regional QM receives a verification task. They review the evidence and mark the CAPA action as Verified.
User action — review and one click
Effectiveness Review Scheduled
The system automatically schedules effectiveness reviews at 30, 60, and 90 days. A task appears in the Regional QM's queue on each date. If the CAPA is effective, both the CAPA and the original NC are closed.
Automatic — Scheduled Flow
Dashboard Updates in Real Time
Switch to the Power BI dashboard. The NC count for Johannesburg has incremented by one. The Enterprise Quality Index has adjusted. The CAPA ageing chart shows a new entry. All of this happened without anyone running a report or updating a spreadsheet.
Automatic — Real-time Dataverse → Power BI

One non-conformance raised. Nine automated actions. Zero emails. Zero manual handoffs. Zero chance of this falling through the cracks.

And every step — every notification, every approval, every piece of evidence — is permanently recorded as an auditable trail for your external ISO auditors.

Key Demo Moment
After showing the Teams notification arrive in real time, say: "This notification appeared in under three seconds. In the current process, this same information would take between two hours and two days to reach the Regional Quality Manager — if it reached them at all."

Scene 6

The Intelligent Enterprise

This scene separates the platform from any standard QMS. The audience should leave this scene thinking "We've never had anything that can do this."
6:30 – 7:30  ·  60 seconds

Stage Direction

Open the AI Insights panel from the left navigation. The screen shows a list of active AI-generated insights, each with a confidence badge, a recommendation, and an explanation of WHY the platform reached that conclusion.

Until now, we've shown you a system that tracks, automates, and reports. But this platform doesn't just record your quality data — it reads it. Let me show you what the platform sees that humans can't.

AI Insight — Pattern Detection
Recurring Non-Conformance: Incoming Goods Inspection
Four centres across two regions have raised non-conformances related to incoming goods inspection failures in the past 60 days. The affected centres are Johannesburg, Pretoria, Durban, and Cape Town. The category "Incoming Goods — Specification Non-Compliance" appeared 11 times across these centres.
Why this matters: The clustering across multiple centres and regions suggests a systemic issue — likely a supplier quality problem or an SOP gap — rather than isolated operational failures. Recommend: Review Supplier XYZ's quality performance; cross-reference with SOP-PRO-042 (Incoming Goods Inspection Procedure) for adequacy.
AI Insight — Risk Prediction
East London Centre: Predicted Quality Decline
The AI model predicts a 78% probability that the East London centre's quality score will drop below 75 (the Red threshold) within the next 90 days. Current trajectory shows declining audit scores, rising NC volume, and three overdue CAPAs.
Why this matters: East London's current quality score is 79.2 — still in the Amber zone. But the leading indicators (NC velocity, CAPA closure rate, training compliance) match the pattern seen at Port Elizabeth before its score dropped to 68 last year. Early intervention now — a focused audit and a CAPA acceleration plan — could prevent the decline.
AI Insight — Clause Prioritisation
ISO Clause 8.5.1 Requires National Attention
Across the enterprise, Clause 8.5.1 (Control of Production and Service Provision) accounts for 28% of all Major audit findings in the current year. This is disproportionate — the next-highest clause accounts for 11%. Finding severity in this clause has increased quarter-on-quarter for three consecutive quarters.
Why this matters: This clause governs your core service delivery process. Its disproportionate share of findings indicates either an SOP gap, a training gap, or a process design issue at the national level. Recommend: Include Clause 8.5.1 as a mandatory focus in every audit in the next programme cycle, and commission a process review of the associated SOPs.
AI Insight — Executive Recommendation
Three Centres Require Executive Intervention
East London, Bloemfontein, and Polokwane have Quality Health Scores below 80, overdue CAPA rates above 40%, and declining trends across multiple indicators. These centres have not responded adequately to regional-level interventions over the past two quarters.
Why this matters: Regional management has flagged improvement plans at each centre, but the data shows these plans are not producing results. The platform recommends escalating to executive-sponsored intervention: a direct review with each Centre Manager, resource reallocation, and an accelerated audit cycle. Confidence: 89%.

This is not a dashboard. This is an early warning system. The platform is reading patterns across 25 centres and 18 months of data to tell you what is about to go wrong — and what to do about it — before it shows up in your lagging indicators.

No spreadsheet analysis can do this. No monthly report can do this. This runs continuously, and it learns as your data grows.

Presenter Notes

Pacing
Spend the most time on Scenes 4 and 6 — the workflow chain and AI insights. These are the moments that create executive conviction. Move briskly through Scene 3 (module walkthrough).
Data
Use realistic mock data from actual centres. Executives will disengage if they see "Lorem Ipsum" or obviously fake centre names. Use real geography, realistic numbers, and plausible scenarios.
Live Notifications
The Teams notification in Scene 4 must appear live. Pre-stage the Power Automate flow so it fires when you submit the NC form. This "live" moment is the most memorable part of the demo.
Silence
Use deliberate pauses. After the cluttered desktop in Scene 1, hold five seconds. After the drill-through in Scene 5, hold three seconds. After the AI recommendations in Scene 6, hold three seconds. Silence is your most powerful tool.
Contrast
The demo is structured as a before/after story. The transition from Scene 1 to Scene 2 must feel dramatic. Everything after that builds on the contrast you established in that first transition.
Fallback
Have screenshots of every screen as backup. If a live demo fails (network, permissions, data), switch to screenshots immediately without apology. Never let the audience see you troubleshoot.
